# Knowledge, Wisdom & Spirituality: The Three-Step Model of Cognition in Lifechanyuan (Academic Analysis)

## Abstract

"Knowledge, Wisdom & Spirituality" is the core proposition in the Lifechanyuan system about human cognitive ability. In essays including *The Three Great Pursuits of Human Life*, *Preface to the Wisdom Essays*, *The Wisdom Formation*, *Transform Knowledge, Abandon Wisdom — Enter the Inner Chamber* and *Gain Clouds the Mind, Wisdom Dims the Heart, the Heart Blinds the Nature*, Xuefeng sets out a three-step model, knowledge → wisdom → spirituality, and gives one formula: knowledge + spirituality = wisdom. This paper sets out the conceptual structure of the model, its textual sources and the levels of wisdom, and compares it briefly with Western epistemology.

---

## 1. Scope and Questions

### 1.1 Objects of Study

- The definitions of knowledge, wisdom and spirituality and how they follow one another
- Two sides of wisdom: needed to open the door of spirituality, and yet able to cloud the heart
- Levels of wisdom (five levels / low-middle-high / lower, middle, great, highest)
- Ways past the three steps and the point where they end: reading the book of heaven, turning knowing and setting cleverness down, letting true nature bloom

### 1.2 Core Questions

1. What grounds the sequence of the three steps? How is "knowledge is not ability" argued?
2. What does "knowledge + spirituality = wisdom" say epistemologically? What does spiritual perception do in it?
3. If knowledge and wisdom are needed to open the door, why "set cleverness down"?
4. Why is spirituality both a later step and something the earlier steps cannot replace?

## 3. Conceptual Structure

### 3.1 The Three-Step Model

```
Knowledge
  ↓ working, applying, thinking
Wisdom
  ↓ drawn up by spiritual perception
Spirituality
```

| Dimension | Knowledge | Wisdom | Spirituality |
|-----------|-----------|--------|--------------|
| Source | Experience handed down (school, books, masters) | Knowledge grown through thinking | Spiritual perception |
| Image | Trunk, branches, leaves | Blossom and fruit | The door of life |
| Ability | Hold the patterns and experience already found | Find, analyse, tell apart, solve; invent and create | Tell Truth, Goodness, Beauty and Love from their opposites; have morality; see the direction of life |
| Relation to morality | None | None | Morality must be worked on in spirituality |

### 3.2 "Knowledge + Spirituality = Wisdom"

The formula says that wisdom does not come from knowledge and reasoning alone. A great deal of knowledge without spiritual perception does not turn into wisdom by itself; rich knowledge rises into wisdom only when drawn up by spiritual perception. An entry in the fifth edition of *New Era Human 800 Concepts* runs in parallel: with spiritual perception and no wisdom, one can sense that the Greatest Creator exists but cannot say exactly how the Dao operates ("knowing that it is so, not why"); with wisdom and no spiritual perception, one can find some secrets but cannot truly sense that the Creator exists ("knowing why it is so, not that it is"). Neither side is complete without the other.

### 3.3 Sequence and Irreplaceability

The three steps go forward in order, and Xuefeng also says:

- without knowledge and wisdom the door of spirituality cannot be opened, and "even if you understand a little, it stays superstition";
- yet there are ways past: reading the book of heaven, or having the affinity to meet a Celestial Being or a Buddha, and then "even a person who cannot read can gain spirituality."

The two statements look opposed. Xuefeng's treatment is that the usual road goes step by step, and the one-step arrival is the exception, available under particular conditions.

### 3.4 Turn Knowing, Set Cleverness Down

"Turn knowing, set cleverness down, open spiritual perception, enter the hall" is where the three steps end. What is "set down" here answers one particular misreading, that wisdom is the source of spirituality: "Wisdom is a product of thought, not the source of spirit." Since knowledge and wisdom are what open the door, "set cleverness down" is read as not being lost in wisdom and not taking wisdom for spirit, and not as opposition to learning.

### 3.5 How Spirituality and Wisdom Relate: One Reading (Exploratory, Not a Conclusion)

"Spirituality is wisdom raised higher" and "knowledge + spirituality = wisdom" can look circular: spirituality is above wisdom, and also a part of it. Here is one way to read them, offered for discussion.

1. **Spirituality is innate to life, only covered by "dust."** The Corpus has matching images: the heart is a mirror, and a mirror under dust shows things unclearly; open spiritual perception and enter the hall; a baby has a pure nature, never lost.
2. **"Spirituality is wisdom raised higher" means that it comes into view.** A person who pursues wisdom, and goes deep, may meet spirituality. "Raised higher" is read here as dust being wiped away and what was always there showing, not as another layer added on top of wisdom. One caution: Xuefeng also says "cleverness clouds the heart." Pursuing wisdom until it turns into pride adds a layer of dust instead. So "may meet" is the safer wording.
3. **"Knowledge + spirituality = wisdom" means participation.** Knowledge is not ability; wisdom is ability. For knowledge to become wisdom, spirituality (drawn up by spiritual perception) must take part.
4. **A corollary: with the same amount of knowledge and the same intelligence, the purer the spirituality, the higher the wisdom.** The less of it is covered (*New Era Human 800 Concepts* speaks of cultivation as perfecting the nonmaterial structure of LIFE), the more efficiently knowledge turns into wisdom.

On this reading the two sentences each cover one end: the first is about where the road from wisdom leads, and the second about what the road from knowledge to wisdom requires. This is one reading of the texts. Xuefeng does not set out the relation between the two words in one place, and other readings are welcome.

## 4. Levels of Wisdom

### 4.1 Five Levels

The wisdom of the physical eye → the heavenly eye → the wisdom eye → the Dharma eye → the Buddha eye. *New Era Human 800 Concepts* gives only the names and their order.

### 4.2 Low, Middle, High

- **Low:** grasping things quickly and flexibly and solving problems (the carrying pole, Cao Chong weighing the elephant, Archimedes testing the crown).
- **Middle:** realising, proving and attaining the Way; freed from life and death; rising into sagehood.
- **High:** great awakening, seeing one's nature, becoming one with the Way.

### 4.3 Lower, Middle, Great, Highest

The lower wisdom is restless, the middle competes, the great seems foolish, the highest has no form; the lower is busy with plans and systems, the middle with laws and regulations, the great with spiritual civilisation, and the highest roams free in the world of the heart.

## 5. A Brief Comparison With Western Epistemology

| Aristotle | Lifechanyuan |
|-----------|--------------|
| episteme (scientific knowledge) | Knowledge |
| phronesis (practical wisdom) | Wisdom |
| nous (intuitive intellect) | Spirituality |

This is a rough mapping. The difference is that Lifechanyuan places spirituality above reason, and says that besides the step-by-step sequence there are direct ways of opening it, "reading the book of heaven" and "affinity with a Celestial Being or Buddha."
