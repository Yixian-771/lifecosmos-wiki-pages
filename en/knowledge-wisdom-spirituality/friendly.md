# Knowledge, Wisdom & Spirituality: Three Steps

## 1. An Ordinary Question

You went to school for years, read many books, worked for many years. Is that enough?

Most people would say no, keep learning.

Xuefeng gives a finer answer. Life, he says, has three pursuits, each one higher than the last:

> Life has three pursuits: knowledge, wisdom and spirituality. These are the three steps to becoming a Celestial Being or a Buddha.

**Knowledge → Wisdom → Spirituality**

---

## 2. Knowledge: Picked Up

> Knowledge is what people have come to know of the material world, and the summing-up of experience.

People deal with the material world, find patterns, and gain in practice some real understanding and experience. Taken together, that is knowledge.

> Knowledge is picked up. It is a summary of experience. It is the trunk, the branches and the leaves.

Knowledge deserves respect:

> With rich knowledge a person leaves primitive ignorance behind and lays the ground for civilisation. So seeking knowledge comes first in life.

But knowledge is not ability. School exists so that we need not try every thing ourselves, and can quickly take up what earlier people worked out:

> Bachelor, master, doctor: what they gain is all knowledge. Knowledge is not ability. It is the precondition and the groundwork for gaining ability.

---

## 3. Wisdom: Grown

> Knowledge is picked up, wisdom is grown. Knowledge is the summing-up of experience, wisdom is the result of thinking. Knowledge is the trunk, the branches and the leaves; wisdom is the fragrant blossom and the rich fruit.

Once you have knowledge, you still have to turn it into ability. Xuefeng gives an everyday picture:

> We buy flour, oil, vegetables and seasoning at the store, and that does not solve hunger. To solve hunger we must work them into food, and that power of working things into food is the power that turns knowledge into wisdom.

What you buy is knowledge. Knowing how to cook is wisdom.

A person with wisdom knows where to put their strength, where to put their mind, and how to use their time. Seeking wealth, Xuefeng says, is not as good as seeking wisdom:

> Wealth is dead, wisdom is alive... Wealth can be stolen, lost or seized, while wisdom stays with its owner for good and lights a whole life.

Wisdom has levels. His example of the lowest: two brothers travel, each carrying a big case, and are tired. The elder buys a carrying pole, hangs both cases from it, and they take turns carrying. The load is light.

> Wisdom has five levels, from low to high: the wisdom of the physical eye, the heavenly eye, the wisdom eye, the Dharma eye, the Buddha eye.

---

## 4. Spirituality: Wisdom Raised Higher

Above wisdom is spirituality. This step matters most, and Xuefeng says two things about it.

**First, without spirituality there is no real wisdom.**

> Rich knowledge can rise into wisdom only when drawn up by spiritual perception. Without spiritual perception, there can be no wisdom. So if wisdom must be defined, it is this: knowledge + spirituality = wisdom.

**Second, wisdom cannot settle a person's final worry.**

> Wisdom can make a life full and rich, but it cannot settle a person's final worry. Only when wisdom rises to the height of spirituality can a person tell Truth, Goodness, Beauty and Love from their opposites, have morality, and see the direction and purpose of life.

Knowledge and wisdom have nothing to do with morality:

> A person with knowledge and wisdom does not necessarily have morality. To have morality you must work on spirituality.

And the other way round:

> Without knowledge and wisdom, the door of spirituality cannot be opened. Even if you understand a little, it stays superstition, and you are still lost, not knowing where to step next.

So not one of the three steps can be skipped.

**How do spirituality and wisdom relate?**

You may find it circular: spirituality is wisdom raised higher, and yet knowledge plus spirituality is wisdom. Here is one reading to think about (an exploration, not a conclusion).

Picture a mirror. A mirror is bright to begin with, and spirituality is like that brightness, something every life has from the start. As time goes on, dust settles on the mirror.

- **Knowledge plus spirituality is wisdom:** with the same knowledge and the same cleverness, the person whose mirror is cleaner can put knowledge to better use, and so has higher wisdom.
- **Spirituality is wisdom raised higher:** a person who pursues wisdom sincerely and goes deep has the dust wiped away layer by layer, and the brightness that was always there shows. But if the pursuit turns into pride, another layer of dust settles. That is "cleverness clouds the heart."

## 5. Is There a Shortcut?

Xuefeng asks it himself: is there a way that needs no school and no books, and opens spiritual perception directly?

His answer: yes. First, whether you can read the book of heaven:

> Read heaven, read earth, read life, read society, understand heaven's revelation from all things in nature, and so communicate with the Greatest Creator directly.

Second, whether you have the affinity to meet a Celestial Being or a Buddha who points the way directly.

> If you have either of these two, even a person who cannot read can gain spirituality and the qualities of a Celestial Being or a Buddha.

He adds:

> A person who often thinks about the final goal of life opens up easily and gains spirituality easily.

---

## 6. Where Wisdom Can Trip You

Wisdom is a good thing, and Xuefeng warns of two things.

**Wisdom can cloud the heart.**

> The heart is a mirror. A bright mirror shows the whole course of things under heaven. If the mirror is under dust, things are seen unclearly... When a person is learned, deep in wisdom, proud of their gifts and looking down on everyone, the heart is clouded.

**Wisdom is neutral.**

> Wisdom is not always Truth, Goodness and Beauty, and does not always bear good fruit.

What it is used for depends on spirituality. The wisdom that benefits humankind, he says, comes from reverence for the Greatest Creator, for life, for nature; the wisdom that harms comes from selfishness, greed and lawlessness.

So his advice is to turn knowing and set cleverness down:

> Wisdom is a product of thought, not the source of spirit... Turn knowing, set cleverness down, open spiritual perception, and you can enter the hall and roam free in boundless LIFE.

"Setting cleverness down" means not being lost inside wisdom, and not taking wisdom for the source of spirit. Knowledge and wisdom are what open the door, so reading goes on as before.

---

## 7. Where It Ends: True Nature

Xuefeng's conclusion is one sentence:

> Do not chase profit, do not show off cleverness, do not lean on the heart. Let your true nature bloom.

His example is the baby:

> A baby has no knowledge and no wisdom, no rules, no hesitation. It cries when it wants to cry and laughs when it wants to laugh. It does not think about power, money, fame or looks, and does not weigh gain against loss. A pure nature, never lost, and so a baby is like a Celestial Being.

---

## Summary

| Step | What it is | What it does | Note |
|------|-----------|--------------|------|
| **Knowledge** | Understanding of the material world and summing-up of experience; picked up | The precondition and groundwork for ability | Not the same as ability |
| **Wisdom** | Knowledge raised higher; grown | Find, analyse, tell apart and solve problems; invent and create | Neutral; can cloud the heart |
| **Spirituality** | Wisdom raised higher | Tell Truth, Goodness, Beauty and Love from their opposites; morality; direction of life | Opening the door takes knowledge and wisdom; it ends in true nature |
